# Sectiō X · The fourth conjugation and -iō verbs

**The one idea to take from this section:** two new kinds of verb, the 4th conjugation (*veniō, venīre*) and a branch of the 3rd (*fugiō, fugere*), both keep an **i** in every form of the present, future, and imperfect. The infinitive tells them apart: long **-īre** is 4th; short **-ere** is 3rd.

With this section the regular conjugations are complete: after it you have the whole present system in the active (present, future, imperfect, and commands) for every regular Latin verb.

## Spot the conjugation, again

| Infinitive | 1st principal part | Conjugation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -āre | -ō | 1st | *vocō, vocāre* |
| -ēre | -eō | 2nd | *videō, vidēre* |
| -ere | -ō | 3rd | *dūcō, dūcere* |
| -ere | **-iō** | 3rd, -iō type | *fugiō, fugere* |
| **-īre** | **-iō** | 4th | *veniō, venīre* |

Two rows have a first principal part in -iō, so the first part alone can't settle it. Look at the infinitive: *venīre*, with a long, stressed ī (ve-NĪ-re), is 4th; *fugere*, with a short, unstressed e (FU-ge-re), is 3rd. Learn both parts of every verb, and say them aloud.

## The three tenses

Here are *veniō*, "come," and *fugiō*, "flee," with *dūcō* from section 8 alongside for comparison.

| | 3rd: *dūcō* | 3rd -iō: *fugiō* | 4th: *veniō* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Present** | | | |
| 1st sg. | dūc**ō** | fug**iō** | ven**iō** |
| 2nd sg. | dūc**is** | fug**is** | ven**īs** |
| 3rd sg. | dūc**it** | fug**it** | ven**it** |
| 1st pl. | dūc**imus** | fug**imus** | ven**īmus** |
| 2nd pl. | dūc**itis** | fug**itis** | ven**ītis** |
| 3rd pl. | dūc**unt** | fug**iunt** | ven**iunt** |
| **Future** | | | |
| 1st sg. | dūc**am** | fug**iam** | ven**iam** |
| 2nd sg. | dūc**ēs** | fug**iēs** | ven**iēs** |
| 3rd sg. | dūc**et** | fug**iet** | ven**iet** |
| 1st pl. | dūc**ēmus** | fug**iēmus** | ven**iēmus** |
| 2nd pl. | dūc**ētis** | fug**iētis** | ven**iētis** |
| 3rd pl. | dūc**ent** | fug**ient** | ven**ient** |
| **Imperfect** | | | |
| 1st sg. | dūc**ēbam** | fug**iēbam** | ven**iēbam** |
| 2nd sg. | dūc**ēbās** | fug**iēbās** | ven**iēbās** |
| 3rd sg. | dūc**ēbat** | fug**iēbat** | ven**iēbat** |
| 1st pl. | dūc**ēbāmus** | fug**iēbāmus** | ven**iēbāmus** |
| 2nd pl. | dūc**ēbātis** | fug**iēbātis** | ven**iēbātis** |
| 3rd pl. | dūc**ēbant** | fug**iēbant** | ven**iēbant** |

Wheelock's models are *audiō* and *capiō*, with *agō* for comparison; every verb in this section's list follows one of the three patterns.

Read the table across and the pattern jumps out: the -iō verb and the 4th-conjugation verb have the **same endings in all three tenses except three present forms**: the 2nd singular and the 1st and 2nd plural (*fugis/venīs*, *fugimus/venīmus*, *fugitis/venītis*). There the 4th conjugation has a long ī and the -iō verb a short i, exactly like *dūcis*, *dūcimus*, *dūcitis*. *Fugit* and *venit* look alike only because a long vowel goes short before final -t (section 1's rule).

### The 4th conjugation, step by step

The stem is the infinitive minus -re, as always: *venī-*. Its long ī shortens before another vowel and before final -t, by the rule you already know.

- **Present:** stem plus the usual endings (*venī-s*, *venī-mus*), except that the 3rd plural is *veniunt*, not *venint*. The -unt is the same one you know from *dūcunt*.
- **Future:** shorten the ī and add the 3rd-conjugation future endings: -am, -ēs, -et, -ēmus, -ētis, -ent. So *veniam*, *veniēs*. As in section 8, there is no -bi-; the future is marked by **ē** in every form but the 1st singular (*veniam*).
- **Imperfect:** -iē- before the familiar -bam, -bās, -bat: *veniēbam*, not *venībam*.

### The -iō verbs: an i that never leaves

A 3rd-conjugation -iō verb is a 3rd-conjugation verb that keeps an i in front of every ending in all three indicative tenses: present, future, and imperfect. Wherever the 4th conjugation has an i, so does the -iō verb, but always short. Where *dūcō* has no i at all, the -iō verb shows one anyway: *fugiunt*, *fugiam*, *fugiēbam*.

A good rule of thumb: in the indicative, **i appears in every form of both types**, and in the imperfect there are always two vowels, **-iē-**, before the -ba-.

## Commands and infinitives

| | 3rd: *dūcō* | 3rd -iō: *fugiō* | 4th: *veniō* |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| infinitive | dūc**ere** | fug**ere** | ven**īre** |
| sg. command | dūc | fug**e** | ven**ī** |
| pl. command | dūc**ite** | fug**ite** | ven**īte** |

The -iō verbs drop their i here and look just like ordinary 3rd-conjugation verbs: *fuge*, *fugite*, *fugere*. The 4th conjugation behaves like the 1st and 2nd: the singular command is the bare stem (*venī!*) and the plural adds -te (*venīte!*).

This section's list contains two of the four short commands from section 8: *dīcō* gives **dīc**, "say!", and *faciō* gives **fac**, "do, make!" Their plurals are regular: *dīcite*, *facite*.

## Sentences

- *Amīcī meī crās venient.* "My friends will come tomorrow."
- *Puellae ex agrō fugiēbant.* "The girls were fleeing from the field."
- *Vēritātem semper dīcēbat.* "He always used to tell the truth."
- *Quid facis?* "What are you doing?"
- *Rōmānī multās terrās capient.* "The Romans will capture many lands."
- *Portam nōn inveniō.* "I don't find the gate."
- *In hāc terrā vīvere possumus.* "We can live in this land."
- *Audīte, discipulī!* "Listen, students!"
- *Timor saepe hominēs superat.* "Fear often overcomes people."
- *Senectūs nōn semper est mala.* "Old age isn't always bad."

## Notes from the word list

- **Faciō** and **capiō** change their a to i inside compounds: *efficiō*, *sufficiō*; *percipiō*, *recipiō*. English keeps the i in "efficient" and "sufficient," and in "percipient" and "recipient."
- **Fugiō** lists **fugitūrum** as its fourth principal part, a form meaning "about to flee," just as *valeō* lists *valitūrum*. "Fugitive" comes from the same root.
- **Dīcō**, "say," and **dūcō**, "lead," differ by a single vowel. So do **via**, "road," and **vīta**, "life." Look twice.
- **Inveniō** is *in* + *veniō*, "come upon," and so "find." English "invent" first meant "find."
- **Vīvō**, "live," is plain 3rd conjugation, like *dūcō*: *vīvis*, *vīvunt*, *vīvam*. It's related to *vīta*.
- **Cum** + ablative means "with": *Cum amīcīs cēnābimus.* "We will dine with friends." As a prefix it shows up as com-, con-, cor-, col-, or just co-, meaning "together," "completely," or simply adding force. You already know one example: *cōnservō*, "keep safe," is *con-* + *servō*. English "collect," "correct," and "cooperate" are others.
- **Quoniam**, "since, because," introduces a reason: *Quoniam perīculum est magnum, fugiēmus.* "Since the danger is great, we will flee."
- **Cupiditās**, **vēritās**, and **voluptās** are 3rd-declension feminines in -tās, -tātis, and **senectūs, senectūtis** is in -tūs, -tūtis, all patterns from section 7. **Timor, timōris** is a masculine -or noun. **Amīcitia**, **hōra**, **nātūra**, and **via** are 1st declension.
- **Beātus** means "happy" in the sense of "fortunate, blessed": *Fēmina beāta est.* "The woman is fortunate."

### Word families

The noun-making recipe from section 8 (fourth principal part, drop -um, add -or) works on these verbs too: *audītum* gives *audītor*, "listener," and *inventum* gives *inventor*, "finder." A second recipe adds **-iō, -iōnis** (3rd-declension feminine) to the same base and names the act itself: *audītiō*, "a hearing," and *dictiō*, "speaking." English keeps them as "audition" and "diction." With a verb's principal parts in hand, you can often guess a dozen related words.

## A reading tip

When you meet a verb with an i before its ending, ask two questions. Is the infinitive -īre or -ere? And is the vowel after the i an **ē** (future or imperfect) or something else (present)? *Venient* is "they will come"; *veniunt* is "they come"; *veniēbant* is "they were coming."

To practice, write out *vīvō*, *fugiō*, and *veniō* in all three tenses, with their commands, and say each form aloud.

## Check yourself

1. Translate *venīs* and *veniēs*.
   - Hint: which one has the future sign?
   - Answer: "you come" and "you will come."
2. Is *faciō, facere* 3rd or 4th conjugation, and how can you tell?
   - Hint: look at the vowel before -re.
   - Answer: 3rd (-iō type): the infinitive has short -ere, not -īre.
3. Give the imperfect, 3rd person plural, of *inveniō*.
   - Hint: two vowels come before -ba-.
   - Answer: *inveniēbant*, "they were finding."
4. Tell one friend "Flee!" and a group of friends "Come!"
   - Hint: -iō verbs drop the i in commands; the 4th conjugation keeps its long ī.
   - Answer: *Fuge!* and *Venīte!*
5. Translate *Dum vīvimus, vēritātem dīcēmus.*
   - Hint: *dīcēmus* has the future sign.
   - Answer: "While we live, we will speak the truth."
