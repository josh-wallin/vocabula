# Sectiō VIII · The third conjugation

**The one idea to take from this section:** an infinitive in short **-ere** marks the 3rd conjugation, and in its future the sign is not -bi- but **-ē-**. *Dūcet* means "he will lead," not "he leads."

You already know two conjugations, the -āre verbs and the -ēre verbs. This section adds a third, whose present system is less tidy than the first two.

## Spot the conjugation from the infinitive

| Infinitive | Stem vowel | Conjugation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| -āre | long ā | 1st | *dēmōnstrāre* |
| -ēre | long ē | 2nd | *docēre* |
| -ere | short e | 3rd | *dūcere* |

A 4th conjugation, with long ī, arrives in section 10.

The only written difference between *docēre* and *dūcere*-type infinitives is a macron, so learn the macron as part of the word. You can hear the difference, too: the long ē of *docēre* carries the stress (do-CĒ-re), while the short e of *dūcere* doesn't (DŪ-ce-re).

## Why this conjugation is irregular

The 3rd conjugation's stem vowel is a short e: *scrībe-*, *dūce-*. Short, unstressed vowels are weak, and over time this one changed or vanished in many forms. The practical answer is to learn the three tenses as patterns. Here is *scrībō*, "to write."

| | Present | Future | Imperfect |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1st sg. | scrīb**ō** | scrīb**am** | scrīb**ēbam** |
| 2nd sg. | scrīb**is** | scrīb**ēs** | scrīb**ēbās** |
| 3rd sg. | scrīb**it** | scrīb**et** | scrīb**ēbat** |
| 1st pl. | scrīb**imus** | scrīb**ēmus** | scrīb**ēbāmus** |
| 2nd pl. | scrīb**itis** | scrīb**ētis** | scrīb**ēbātis** |
| 3rd pl. | scrīb**unt** | scrīb**ent** | scrīb**ēbant** |
| English | I write | I will write | I was writing |

Wheelock's model is *agō*; every 3rd-conjugation verb in this section's list follows the same pattern (*agis, agit*; *agam, agēs*; *agēbam*).

### The present: ō, is, it, imus, itis, unt

Following section 1's rule, you'd expect the stem *scrībe-* plus endings. Instead the e disappears before -ō, turns into i in the next four forms, and into u before -nt. That vowel sequence (ō, i, i, i, i, u) is the same one you chant for the 1st/2nd-conjugation future: -bō, -bis, -bit, -bimus, -bitis, -bunt. Same rhythm, different job.

### The future: -am, then -ē-

The 3rd conjugation doesn't use -bi- at all. Its future marker is **ē**, which appears in every form except the 1st person singular, where the ending is -am. By section 1's rule the ē goes short before final -t and before -nt: *scrībet*, *scrībent*. The stem vowel itself has merged away.

This is where most mistakes happen. *Scrībet* and *dūcet* look like 2nd-conjugation presents such as *docet* or *videt*, and they are not:

| Form | Verb | Conjugation | Means |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| *docet* | *doceō, docēre* | 2nd | he **teaches** |
| *dūcet* | *dūcō, dūcere* | 3rd | he **will lead** |
| *manent* | *maneō, manēre* | 2nd | they **stay** |
| *gerent* | *gerō, gerere* | 3rd | they **will carry on** |

The only way to tell them apart is to know which conjugation each verb belongs to, which is one more reason to learn the infinitive with every verb.

### The imperfect: -ēbam

The imperfect is the friendly tense: it works just like the 1st and 2nd conjugations, present stem plus -bam, -bās, -bat, except that the stem vowel is lengthened to ē. *Scrībēbam* matches *habēbam* exactly.

## Commands

The singular imperative is the present stem, as section 1 taught: *scrībe!* "write!", *vince!* "win!" In the plural, the short e shifts to i, as it does in the present: *scrībite!*, *vincite!* (never *scrībete*).

| Verb | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| *scrībō, scrībere* | *scrībe* | *scrībite* |
| *trahō, trahere* | *trahe* | *trahite* |
| *dūcō, dūcere* | **dūc** | *dūcite* |

Four common verbs drop the final -e in the singular. *Dūc*, "lead!", is the one in this list. The other three belong to verbs you'll learn later: *dīc*, "say!"; *fac*, "do, make!"; and *fer*, "carry!" Memorize these four as exceptions; every other verb follows *scrībe*. (*Fer*'s plural is *ferte*; it comes with *ferō* later.)

## Sentences

- *Cicerō librum novum scrībet.* "Cicero will write a new book." (*Cicerō, Cicerōnis*, m.)
- *Frāter meus multa carmina scrībēbat.* "My brother used to write many poems."
- *Rōmānī bellum gerēbant.* "The Romans were waging war."
- *Cōpiās ex agrīs dūcēmus.* "We will lead the troops out of the fields."
- *Magistra docet, et discipulae discunt.* "The teacher teaches, and the students learn."
- *Ratiō nōn semper vincit.* "Reason doesn't always win."
- *Virtūs vincet.* "Courage will win."

## "To": dative or *ad*?

English uses "to" for two different ideas, and Latin keeps them apart.

- **Giving, showing, telling**: the person on the receiving end is an indirect object, so use the dative. *Sorōrī litterās scrībō.* "I am writing a letter to my sister."
- **Motion toward a place**: use *ad* with the accusative. *Corpus ad agrum trahēbant.* "They were dragging the body to the field."

## Notes from the word list

- **Ex** and **ē** are one preposition, "out of, from," with the ablative. *Ex* can stand before any sound; *ē* only before consonants, rather like English "an" and "a": *ex agrīs*, *ē caelō* or *ex caelō*. After a number, *ex* means "of." Both forms are common prefixes, sometimes changed to match the next sound.
- **Ad** + accusative, "to, toward, near," also works as a prefix whose d often changes to match the next consonant: English "accept," "appeal."
- **Agō** has a wide range: "drive, lead, do, act," and "spend" (time or a life). *Vītam bonam agit.* "He leads a good life." The idiom **grātiās agere** with a dative means "to thank": *Cicerōnī grātiās agimus.* "We thank Cicero."
- **Gerō** is "carry," but most often "carry on" or "wage": *bellum gerere*, "to wage war."
- **Dum**, "while," with the indicative: *Dum puerī cēnant, magister scrībit.* "While the boys are dining, the teacher is writing." (With a different verb mood, which comes much later, it means "until.")
- **Tamen**, "nevertheless": *Perīculum est magnum; tamen manēbimus.* "The danger is great; still, we will stay."
- **Numquam**, "never": *Sine cōpiīs numquam vincēmus.* "Without supplies we will never win."
- **Discō** lists only three principal parts: *discō, discere, didicī*.
- **Laus**, "praise, glory," and **lībertās**, "liberty," are 3rd-declension feminines; **victōria** and **cōpia** are 1st. *Cōpia* is "supply," but the plural *cōpiae* is "supplies" or "troops, forces."

### Who does it: nouns in -tor

Take the fourth principal part of a verb, drop the -um, and add -or, and you get a noun for the person who does the action. *Scrībō* gives *scrīptum*, so *scrīptor* is "writer." The same recipe turns *doctum* into *doctor*, "teacher," *āctum* into *āctor*, "doer, actor," and *victum* into *victor*, "winner." Spotting a verb inside a noun will often give you its meaning for free, as *discere*, "to learn," sits inside *discipulus*, "learner."

## A reading tip

When a verb ends in -ēs, -et, -ēmus, -ētis, or -ent, stop and ask which conjugation it belongs to before you translate. If the infinitive has short -ere, it's future.

Practice by conjugating one 1st-, one 2nd-, and one 3rd-conjugation verb in all three tenses.

## Check yourself

1. Translate *Discipulī multa discent.*
   - Hint: what is the infinitive of *discō*?
   - Answer: "The students will learn many things." *Discere* is 3rd conjugation, so -ent is future.
2. Translate *Magister puerōs docet* and *Rēx cōpiās dūcet*, and explain the difference in tense.
   - Hint: compare *docēre* and *dūcere*.
   - Answer: "The teacher teaches the boys" (present, 2nd conj.) and "The king will lead the troops" (future, 3rd conj.).
3. Give the imperfect, 3rd person plural, of *trahō*.
   - Hint: present stem with a long ē, then -bant.
   - Answer: *trahēbant*, "they were dragging."
4. Tell a group of students "Write!"
   - Hint: in the plural imperative the short e becomes i.
   - Answer: *Scrībite!*
5. How would you say "to the field" after a verb of motion, and why not the dative?
   - Hint: is anyone receiving something?
   - Answer: *ad agrum*: it's motion toward a place, not an indirect object.
